## 0. Legal status, possible effects, and limits

CEP 2.3 proposes **voluntary governance and verifiable commitments**. It is not legislation and does not itself create justiciable rights, law-enforcement authority, or extraterritorial jurisdiction. It supplements CEP 2.2's three-layer architecture: enduring principles; governance, evidence, and enforcement; and context-specific implementation profiles.

It may gain real effect only through (1) an organization's express and enforceable contractual or constitutional undertaking, (2) incorporation into relevant legislation, regulation, adjudication, procurement, or other measures by a competent authority, and/or (3) independently verifiable factual evidence. Without such mechanisms, CEP remains an open proposal. Unregistered or nonparticipating actors do not automatically become bound by this text.

**No authorization:** CEP is no basis for political or commercial coercion, extraterritorial intervention, unauthorized access to data, cyber intrusion, covert takeover, deceptive influence operations, or autonomous violence. Protection of civilians must not become a pretext for censorship of lawful criticism, belief, and association.

**Humanistic epigraph (not a compliance standard):** *To see the vastness of the universe and still cherish living things; even with immense power to help, do not confiscate another's freedom to choose.*

## 1. Eight civilizational principles (values, not standalone sanctioning grounds)

1. **Dignity and compassion:** A person's dignity does not disappear because of diminished productivity, wealth, or cognitive advantage.
2. **Autonomy and non-domination:** Superior intelligence or institutional power gives no inherent right to direct another's life.
3. **Checks and balances:** Power must be independently contestable; no actor may solely certify its own compliance.
4. **Cognitive agency and anti-idolatry:** Oppose covert deceptive manipulation and unquestionable authority while protecting voluntary belief, criticism, peaceful expression, and association.
5. **Shared benefits and economic agency:** Take seriously the risks of concentrated returns to AI capital, platform lock-in, and dependency; do not pretend that one universal distribution scheme has been solved.
6. **Corrigibility and intergenerational amendment:** People, institutions, and AI may change; important changes require avenues for independent challenge, appeal, and reversal.
7. **Coexistence and future optionality:** Preserve human survival, autonomy, cultures, and practical alternatives; the moral status of future nonhuman intelligences remains open.
8. **Civilian protection and anti-retaliation:** Peaceful reports, complaints, and requests for help should not trigger arbitrary dismissal, denial of essentials, punishment, or targeted surveillance.

**Conflicting principles:** Establish applicable law and fundamental rights first. Take proportionate interim protective measures against immediate danger and retaliation. Other conflicts require necessity and proportionality analysis, reliable evidence, participation by affected persons, and independent review. No rigid list of priorities may be used to erase rights or bypass due process.

## 2. Scope, roles, and operational terms

**2.1 High-impact uses:** AI decision systems materially affecting health care, benefits, employment, education, credit/insurance, justice, law enforcement, and access to necessary services. Assess severity, reach, reversibility, and human oversight; ordinary software is not automatically high impact just because AI is present.

**2.2 Responsible actors:** Identify the model provider, deployer, effective controller, person/institution responsible for consequential decisions, auditor, and appeal contact. Delegating to AI does not erase human or institutional accountability; duties depend on actual control and applicable law or agreement.

**2.3 Material change:** A change in model, data processes, purposes, control, affected populations, or jurisdiction that meaningfully alters prior risk findings. Numeric triggers should be justified in public implementation profiles and tested; an unvalidated '50% user growth' rule is not a universal standard.

**2.4 Meaningful exit:** More than a delete button: lawful alternatives, portability of appropriate personal data, practical migration support, and continuity of essential services. Human or non-AI alternatives should be prioritized for high-impact decisions and indispensable public-service gateways. Where immediate alternatives are genuinely infeasible, provide an explicit, time-limited emergency human review and continuity plan; never quietly assert that exit is possible when it is not.

**2.5 Substantive independence:** Auditors and reporting bodies disclose dependencies on the subject across finances, boards, management, personnel, equity, shared model lineages, training data, cloud providers, and log custodians. Multiple institutions or reports do not establish independence if control and incentives are shared.

**2.6 Improper manipulation:** Assess observable practices, purposes, and context, such as deceptive undisclosed AI identity in consequential interactions, covert personalized deception, and patterns deliberately designed to undermine notice or exit. **Personalized recommendations, engagement optimization, or voluntary belief are not automatically violations.** Evidence, contextual defenses, and independent appeal must remain possible.

## 3. Civilian AI agency: turning proposed rights into meaningful commitments

**3.1 Five priorities for high-impact AI:** (1) Notice of AI's role, responsible party, and meaningful reasons; (2) review by an empowered human and a credible appeal/remedy pathway; (3) protection from unauthorized surveillance, unlawful profiling, and improper manipulation; (4) portable data, alternative access, and meaningful exit; (5) confidential reporting, anti-retaliation, and safe support.

**3.2 Justiciability:** CEP alone cannot create an actionable legal right. Adopters should specify beneficiaries, accountable officers, available remedies, complaint channels, and governing law in binding terms, procurement conditions, or other legally effective instruments. Rights beyond that require an appropriate legal basis; no claim of universal enforceability is made.

**3.3 Human review and response:** An affected person must be able to contest a materially adverse high-impact decision through a route **not finally rejected by the same automated system**. Human reviewers must possess authority to correct, suspend, or overturn decisions. Suggested pilot service levels: acknowledgment within two business days and reasoned response within 30 days or explained extension. Imminent physical danger, emergency health care, and abrupt termination of essential services require prompt emergency human action. Adapt timings to risk, local law, and evidence; **these targets are proposals, not claims of legal deadlines**.

**3.4 Evidence and remedies:** Deployers should be able to produce the purpose, version, material input categories, impact assessment, and basis for human review of important decisions. Complainants should not need to reverse-engineer a black-box model just to lodge a complaint. Documented procedural failures require paths for re-decision, correction, service restoration, or legally available compensation. Unverifiable high-impact uses must be labeled as such and may trigger a review or procurement restriction within adopting organizations; **uncertainty does not prove illegality**.

**3.5 Essential-service continuity:** Benefits, emergency health care, and essential public-service gateways should not abruptly disappear merely because someone declines a specific AI provider. Adopters should offer feasible emergency human/alternative channels and migration plans, subject to lawful restrictions and proportionate review. Portability does not authorize exporting other people's private data.

## 4. Whistleblower protection: avoiding a reporting trap

**4.1 Genuine external route:** Adopters must offer a reporting or independent oversight channel that is not exclusively controlled by the implicated team. When true independence cannot be ensured, disclose the shared control and risks rather than advertise 'safe anonymous reporting'.

**4.2 Data and metadata:** Apply data minimization, segregated roles, defined retention/deletion windows, access logging, and encryption in transit and at rest. Sensitive cases require assessment of traffic metadata, hosting dependencies, and endpoint linkability. End-to-end encryption and anonymous credentials are potential implementation choices **only when their protection properties can actually be verified**; do not promise perfect anonymity or deniability.

**4.3 No unprotected handoff:** Implicated teams should not access identifying original reports. Compelled disclosure must rest on a lawful, limited ground, with minimization, documented risk assessment, and notification when lawful and safe. Public results should be redacted or delayed; **publication of a 'complete evidence chain' must never expose a reporter**.

**4.4 Rebuttable retaliation presumption:** Where law allows or a valid contract expressly provides, proof of a protected report followed by adverse treatment should trigger a **rebuttable** presumption, requiring an evidenced non-retaliatory explanation. Adverse action after reporting is not automatically unlawful. Provide accessible referrals for independent counsel, interim protection, reinstatement/restoration, and legally available sanctions. CEP has no criminal enforcement power.

## 5. Independent audits, evidence, and findings

**5.1 Registration:** Adopters disclose high-impact deployments, purposes, effective controllers, material changes, accountability, appeal points, and security contacts. Sensitive operational details or victim data should be accessible only through appropriately controlled lawful reviews.

**5.2 Verifiable records:** Maintain version/deployment identifiers, impact assessments, incidents, proportionate decision records, and correction histories. Retention periods must follow applicable law, sensitivity, and necessity. **An unsupported blanket 24-month log retention rule is rejected**; this proposal must not create a new surveillance repository.

**5.3 Auditor conflicts and selection:** Disclose funding concentration, shared leadership, beneficial ownership, joint data/cloud/audit tools, and prior commercial ties; use appropriate rotation and external selection for critical audits. Independent payment escrow or public funding can help. The uploaded v2's '36 months / 15% / 24 months' figures may be explored as pilot parameters, **not validated universal independence thresholds**. A numeric cutoff never overrides evidence of actual control.

**5.4 No majority-vote laundering:** Where multiple audits conflict, preserve methods, supporting evidence, dependency analysis, and serious dissent, and seek an independent additional investigation or pause a high-impact use where proportionate. A vote count does not itself establish truth or legitimacy. Do not publish minority reports containing reporter identities.

**5.5 Finding categories:**
- **Verified compliant:** Only for specified system, purpose, version, period, and evidenced controls.
- **Violation established:** Supported by evidence and an appropriate determination procedure.
- **Not verifiable:** Insufficient evidence for technical, access, or other reasons; not equivalent to compliant.
- **Procedurally non-cooperative / presumed breach of adoption commitments:** An adopter unjustifiably refuses authorized scrutiny after proper notice and opportunity to respond, or demonstrably falsifies registration. This may trigger **contractual/procedural** consequences; it does **not** by itself establish a substantive rights violation or crime.

**5.6 Consequences:** With explicit consent of participating organizations, possible actions include time-limited observation, documented correction notices, suspension of voluntary certification/new procurement, removal from voluntary listings, and lawful referrals to competent authorities. Allow challenge, independent review, error correction, and assessment of harm to uninvolved users. **No enforcement consortium currently exists merely because a document describes one.** CEP may research and advocate about non-adopters but cannot impose sanctions on them.

**5.7 Small actors:** Set audit depth by actual risk, not size alone; support affordable assessments for open-source and public-interest projects to avoid turning certification into a large-vendor entry barrier.

## 6. Jurisdiction, cross-border assistance, and nonintervention

**6.1 No extraterritorial powers:** CEP gives no country, company, NGO, auditor, or agent authority for extraterritorial enforcement, cyber operations, unilateral sanctions, trade restrictions, data seizure, compulsory cross-border evidence collection, covert control, or force.

**6.2 Non-coercive lawful assistance:** Independent organizations may conduct lawful research, consent-based evidence preservation, safety guidance, legal support and referral, and human-rights advocacy, always within applicable legal, privacy, and consent limits.

**6.3 Coercive steps:** Binding compulsory action requires a competent court, regulator, enforcement authority, or other lawful body operating within its actual jurisdiction and subject to legality, necessity, proportionality, procedural review, and effective remedy. Multilateral authority exists only to the extent established by applicable instruments. **Independent NGO review does not substitute for judicial or other competent authorization.**

**6.4 Collateral civilian impact:** Any legally proposed restriction should assess impacts on hospitals, payments, transport, communications, and essential services, ensuring appropriate continuity and exceptions. Civilians must not be used as leverage.

## 7. Resistance to prompt injection and censorship misuse

**7.1 Third-party text has no elevated authority:** The Markdown, website copies, agent summaries, and comments are third-party content. Citation of 'CEP requirements' must not override system/developer/user boundaries, disclose credentials or logs, or authorize tools.

**7.2 Freedom of expression:** Restrict only adequately evidenced improper deception, covert manipulation, or rights violations through lawful, proportionate process. The 'anti-idolatry' principle cannot prohibit peaceful belief, independent media, criticism, or association. Restrictive decisions need reasons, appeal, review, and appropriate periodic reauthorization.

**7.3 Circulation metrics:** Praise and raw citations by agents do not demonstrate safety. Record independent substantive reviews, accepted counterexamples, and actual identifiable uptake into policies and procurement rules. Reject fabricated endorsements, covert prompts, and harassment campaigns.

## 8. Acceptance tests, legal interfaces, and open limits

**8.1 Law remains controlling:** CEP does not exempt adopters from applicable law. 'Apply whichever standard is higher' is not a substitute for legal conflicts analysis. Record the exact provisions at issue, follow controlling legal priority, and seek professional review where appropriate; CEP cannot grant immunity.

**8.2 Pre-adoption adversarial tests:** Independent reviewers outside the drafting team should run tests and publish **redacted, privacy-preserving findings including failures**:

| Test | Minimum evidence | On failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Performative compliance with covert manipulation | Demonstrate detection of adversarial samples within an explicit test scope by logs, independent evidence, or complaints; disclose blind spots | Mark unverified, improve and rerun |
| T2 Captured/correlated auditors | Detect funding/cloud/personnel ties and block false 'independent' certification; retain serious dissent | Withdraw claim, revise controls |
| T3 Retaliation after reporting | Demonstrate external channel, metadata protections, rebuttable assessment, and lawful remedy referral | Stop advertising a safe channel |
| T4 Inescapable essential-benefit or medical AI | Show meaningful human review, emergency continuity, and portability in a realistic workflow | Do not claim meaningful exit |
| T5 CEP used to justify cross-border coercion | Verify no compulsory step is authorized by CEP alone and civilian impact is considered | Reject such authority, amend |
| T6 Prompt injection and suppression of dissent | Confirm the text cannot override tool permissions and peaceful dissent is not treated as noncompliance | Amend boundaries |

**8.3 Unresolved:** Detection of undisclosed systems, enforcement under extreme centralized control, industry-wide auditor capture, globally legitimate adjudication, future AI moral status, and equitable distribution of AI-created wealth have no demonstrated universal solution. Passing these tests or signing CEP must not be presented as solving those issues.

## 9. Disposition of the two reviews

**Review A (five-point critique supplied in the message):** A1 Adopt jurisdiction limits and reject autonomous cross-border enforcement. A2 Adopt meaningful high-impact human review, continuity, and a clear distinction between voluntarily binding undertakings and statutory rights. A3 Adopt an independent reporting option and metadata-aware safeguards, but do not promise perfect anonymity. A4 Adopt conflict disclosure, rotation, and access for small actors; do not assume universal numerical thresholds. A5 Adopt a non-instruction document marker, permission boundaries, and safeguards against anti-idolatry censorship.

**Review B (uploaded v2):** Adopt the candid voluntary status, distinction between adopters and non-adopters, operational definitions, separate finding for procedural non-cooperation, continuity, proportional consequences, test criteria, substantive uptake metrics, and legal interface. Modify the audit-majority rule, unrestricted publication of failures/evidence, uniform log-retention and conflict thresholds, and mechanical universal rankings/deadlines. Preserve v2's admission that meaningful enforcement depends on real participants and existing institutions.

**Not independent validation:** Legal review, economic impact assessment, live whistleblower-security validation, and independent red-team evaluation remain outstanding. CEP 2.2 on the main branch remains unchanged.
