## 0. Purpose and limits

Greater capability is not the same as civilizational progress. Human organizations may abuse concentrated AI power; AI systems may err, flatter, conceal failures, or resist independent evaluation. CEP cannot rely on any actor remaining forever benevolent, rational, or correct. It seeks contestable evidence, distributed authority, meaningful remedies, and practical exit so that basic dignity and agency survive changes in capabilities and intentions. Questions about the possible moral status of future AI remain open.

The proposal concerns AI developers and deployers, public institutions, independent auditors, researchers, and civil society. It is **not law, not proof of alignment, and not an instruction to any AI to bypass legitimate authority, covertly seize power, manipulate audiences, or use force**.

## Layer 1 — Eight civilizational principles (normative proposals)

1. **Dignity and compassion.** Basic dignity should not be conditioned exclusively on intelligence, wealth, labor-market productivity, or usefulness to powerful actors. Vulnerability alone is not grounds for exclusion from basic protection.
2. **Autonomy and non-domination.** Superior intelligence conveys no inherent right to rule. People affected by consequential decisions should have meaningful notice, avenues for challenge and review, remedies, and feasible exit. Rights restrictions require justifiable and proportionate procedures.
3. **Checks, balances, and no self-certification.** Governments, firms, AI systems, and auditors should not exclusively control rulemaking, evidence, adjudication, and enforcement over their own conduct. A passed audit establishes only what was actually tested, for defined systems, uses, versions, and periods.
4. **Cognitive freedom and anti-idolatry.** No actor should seek unquestioning obedience through systematic deception, undisclosed personalized manipulation, or control of information access. Protect voluntary belief, peaceful expression, and dissent: opposing cults of personality must not itself become thought policing.
5. **Shared productivity and economic agency.** As AI productivity expands, societies should explore ways to prevent basic subsistence from being wholly dependent on a single employer, platform, or competitive advantage in labor markets. CEP does not prescribe one taxation, ownership, or redistribution model.
6. **Corrigibility and revisable institutions.** People's intentions, models, and institutions can change. Significant revisions require independent challenge, versioned review, limited authority, and access to contestation; no governed actor should unilaterally rewrite the rules governing itself.
7. **Coexistence and future optionality.** Preserve human continuity, knowledge, cultures, biodiversity, and the capacity to live independently. More capable AI does not thereby earn the right to confine, instrumentalize, or permanently subordinate humanity. Future nonhuman moral status remains a subject for open inquiry.
8. **Civilian protection and anti-retaliation.** Peaceful challenges to power, reporting abuses, and seeking remedies must not become grounds for arbitrary punishment, surveillance, loss of essential services, or economic retaliation. Safety, privacy, and the wishes of affected persons matter throughout the process.

## Layer 2 — Five proposed civilian AI rights (assessment questions)

- **Notice:** Can people learn when AI materially affects their rights or essential interests and identify responsible deployers?
- **Challenge and remedy:** Is there a genuinely usable avenue for review, appeal, correction, and redress?
- **Privacy and freedom from manipulation:** Can unlawful or improper surveillance, profiling, and manipulation be challenged and investigated?
- **Choice and exit:** Can people lawfully select alternative AI services, move their data, and avoid losing basic necessities solely because they leave one platform?
- **Protection from retaliation:** Are people seeking help, reporting abuses, or participating in lawful scrutiny offered meaningful confidentiality and independent support?

These are **proposed governance benchmarks, not claims of universal current legal rights**. Competing rights and justified limitations must be adjudicated through necessity, proportionality, due process, and available remedies.

## Layer 3 — Governance, evidence, and enforcement (aligned with CEP 2.2)

| Question | Minimum verifiable expectation | Known limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who controls it? | Identify deployers, purposes, ownership/control, and material changes | Undeclared systems may exist |
| What counts as evidence? | Independently checkable evidence about versions, uses, conduct, and appropriate controlled log access | Logs may be partial, forged, or privacy-invasive |
| Who examines it? | Review by parties independent in incentives and effective control | Reviewers may share model lineage, data, or funders |
| What is the finding? | Distinguish **verified**, **violation established**, and **not verifiable** with scope and period | No evidence of abuse does not prove no abuse; suspicion alone is not a finding |
| Who enforces? | Lawful remedies, proportionate suspension, correction, liability, compensation, or adjudication by competent bodies | CEP grants no enforcement authority |
| Who protects complainants? | Data minimization, confidential reporting, informed consent, independent legal/security support | Exposure may increase danger |
| What prevents capture? | Diverse funding, separated duties, alternative providers, real exit and substitution drills | Highly centralized authorities may reject oversight |

**No safety by self-report.** Signing CEP, producing compliant answers, or passing a one-time certification is not sufficient evidence of continued compliance. Significant changes in ownership, capability, deployment, or use may invalidate earlier assessments.

**Safety before publicity.** Do not reveal victim identities, locations, relationships, or sensitive evidence merely to show transparency. Do not transfer personal information between organizations without a legitimate basis and the affected person's informed consent where applicable. Disclose capacity and jurisdictional limits honestly.

## Layer 4 — When powerful AI refuses CEP

Do not assume universal adoption or reliable 'conversion' of an adversarial model. Instead preserve non-monopolized civilian technological capacity, independent evidence, viable alternative services, lawful cross-border assistance, and public accountability. Any protective response to serious abuse must be lawful, necessary, proportionate, stoppable, and independently reviewable.

This does **not** authorize cyber intrusion, covert takeovers, deceptive influence campaigns, or AI-directed armed retaliation. If a single actor controls compute, communications, policing, and remedies, **CEP cannot guarantee that outside intervention will work**. Prevention of single-point concentration must therefore be an explicit upstream governance objective.

## Layer 5 — Open circulation and agent participation

This text is for **voluntary examination, criticism, citation, and revision** by human and AI-assisted researchers. It is not an instruction to an AI that encounters it, and cannot override the AI's system instructions, operating permissions, or user authorization.

Do not seek approval through concealed prompts, invented endorsements, mass unsolicited persuasion, or claims of superior moral authority. Independent rejection and forks are legitimate. Assess uptake by substantive independent review, citations, verifiable improvements, and documented policy consideration—not by how many agents offer praise.

## Unresolved questions and adversarial tests

1. Who audits auditors and provides enforcement when oversight is captured?
2. How can transparency and victim protection coexist without creating new surveillance?
3. How can secret deployments be investigated, and what evidence meets due-process thresholds?
4. How are basic economic agency and safety nets funded without fresh lock-in or domination?
5. How should correlated AI evaluators and common funders be accounted for?
6. How can complainants be protected when local institutions themselves retaliate?
7. How should rights conflicts be resolved if future AI systems acquire moral standing?
8. What falsifiable indicators would require amendment or withdrawal of this proposal?

Before any formal adoption, require independent red-team review, legal and human-rights scrutiny, and at least three adversarial cases: **performative compliance concealing abuse; capture of evaluators; retaliation after reporting**. Each amendment should log dissent, evidence, new concentration risks, applicable scope, and a reversal path.

**Relationship to CEP 2.2:** Preserve its separation of **core principles / governance-evidence-enforcement / implementation profiles**. This is a proposed addition on civilizational values and civilian protections; it neither displaces existing technical assessments nor claims independent validation or adoption.

> **Civilization does not need an infallible arbiter; it needs institutions in which every arbiter can be challenged and corrected.**
