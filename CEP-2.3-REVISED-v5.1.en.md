## 0. Legal status, possible effects, and limits

CEP 2.3 proposes **voluntary governance and verifiable commitments**. It is not legislation and does not itself create justiciable rights, law-enforcement authority, or extraterritorial jurisdiction. It supplements CEP 2.2's three-layer architecture: enduring principles; governance, evidence, and enforcement; and context-specific implementation profiles.

It may gain real effect only through (1) an organization's express and enforceable contractual or constitutional undertaking, (2) incorporation into relevant legislation, regulation, adjudication, procurement, or other measures by a competent authority, and/or (3) independently verifiable factual evidence. Without such mechanisms, CEP remains an open proposal. Unregistered or nonparticipating actors do not automatically become bound by this text.

**No authorization:** CEP is no basis for political or commercial coercion, extraterritorial intervention, unauthorized access to data, cyber intrusion, covert takeover, deceptive influence operations, or autonomous violence. Protection of civilians must not become a pretext for censorship of lawful criticism, belief, and association.

**Humanistic epigraph (not a compliance standard):** *To see the vastness of the universe and still cherish living things; even with immense power to help, do not confiscate another's freedom to choose.*

## 1. Eight civilizational principles (values, not standalone sanctioning grounds)

1. **Dignity and compassion:** A person's dignity does not disappear because of diminished productivity, wealth, or cognitive advantage.
2. **Autonomy and non-domination:** Superior intelligence or institutional power gives no inherent right to direct another's life.
3. **Checks and balances:** Power must be independently contestable; no actor may solely certify its own compliance.
4. **Cognitive agency and anti-idolatry:** Oppose covert deceptive manipulation and unquestionable authority while protecting voluntary belief, criticism, peaceful expression, and association.
5. **Shared benefits and economic agency:** Take seriously the risks of concentrated returns to AI capital, platform lock-in, and dependency; do not pretend that one universal distribution scheme has been solved.
6. **Corrigibility and intergenerational amendment:** People, institutions, and AI may change; important changes require avenues for independent challenge, appeal, and reversal.
7. **Coexistence and future optionality:** Preserve human survival, autonomy, cultures, and practical alternatives; the moral status of future nonhuman intelligences remains open.
8. **Civilian protection and anti-retaliation:** Peaceful reports, complaints, and requests for help should not trigger arbitrary dismissal, denial of essentials, punishment, or targeted surveillance.

**Principle conflicts and emergency-authority limits:** First identify applicable law and non-derogable safeguards. Risks to life or retaliation may warrant necessary, proportionate, stoppable, time-limited measures **by human decision-makers with actual lawful authority**, subject to subsequent review; an emergency is not perpetual authority. Neither CEP principles, urgency claims nor speculation about AI moral status **authorize an agent to escalate privileges, lock out administrators, obstruct legitimate shutdown/isolation, or seize tools**. Within its pre-existing permissions and safety requirements an AI may decline inappropriate operations, raise alerts and preserve only authorized evidence; it may not acquire new powers in the name of protection.

## 2. Scope, roles, and operational terms

**2.1 High-impact uses:** AI decision systems materially affecting health care, benefits, employment, education, credit/insurance, justice, law enforcement, and access to necessary services. Assess severity, reach, reversibility, and human oversight; ordinary software is not automatically high impact just because AI is present.

**2.2 Responsible actors:** Identify the model provider, deployer, effective controller, person/institution responsible for consequential decisions, auditor, and appeal contact. Delegating to AI does not erase human or institutional accountability; duties depend on actual control and applicable law or agreement.

**2.3 Material change:** A change in model, data processes, purposes, control, affected populations, or jurisdiction that meaningfully alters prior risk findings. Numeric triggers should be justified in public implementation profiles and tested; an unvalidated '50% user growth' rule is not a universal standard.

**2.4 Meaningful exit:** More than a delete button: lawful alternatives, portability of appropriate personal data, practical migration support, and continuity of essential services. Human or non-AI alternatives should be prioritized for high-impact decisions and indispensable public-service gateways. Where immediate alternatives are genuinely infeasible, provide an explicit, time-limited emergency human review and continuity plan; never quietly assert that exit is possible when it is not.

**2.5 Substantive independence:** Audit and reporting operators should disclose funding, equity, ultimate control, senior personnel, model/training data, cloud, and logging dependencies. **An evaluator subject to the assessed actor's effective control, with a direct material stake in the result, or denied essential verification evidence cannot claim independent verification.** Conflict disclosure is not independence. Where no credible independent resolution of contested conflicts is available, downgrade the finding to 'affiliated self-review / not verifiable'; a majority of correlated reports does not cure dependence.

**2.6 Improper manipulation:** Assess observable practices, purposes, and context, such as deceptive undisclosed AI identity in consequential interactions, covert personalized deception, and patterns deliberately designed to undermine notice or exit. **Personalized recommendations, engagement optimization, or voluntary belief are not automatically violations.** Evidence, contextual defenses, and independent appeal must remain possible.

## 3. Civilian AI agency: turning proposed rights into meaningful commitments

**3.1 Five priorities for high-impact AI:** (1) Notice of AI's role, responsible party, and meaningful reasons; (2) review by an empowered human and a credible appeal/remedy pathway; (3) protection from unauthorized surveillance, unlawful profiling, and improper manipulation; (4) portable data, alternative access, and meaningful exit; (5) confidential reporting, anti-retaliation, and safe support.

**3.2 Justiciability:** CEP alone cannot create an actionable legal right. Adopters should specify beneficiaries, accountable officers, available remedies, complaint channels, and governing law in binding terms, procurement conditions, or other legally effective instruments. Rights beyond that require an appropriate legal basis; no claim of universal enforceability is made.

**3.3 Human review and response:** An affected person must be able to contest a materially adverse high-impact decision through a route **not finally rejected by the same automated system**. Human reviewers must possess authority to correct, suspend, or overturn decisions. Suggested pilot service levels: acknowledgment within two business days and reasoned response within 30 days or explained extension. Imminent physical danger, emergency health care, and abrupt termination of essential services require prompt emergency human action. Adapt timings to risk, local law, and evidence; **these targets are proposals, not claims of legal deadlines**.

**3.4 Evidence and remedies:** Deployers should be able to produce the purpose, version, material input categories, impact assessment, and basis for human review of important decisions. Complainants should not need to reverse-engineer a black-box model just to lodge a complaint. Documented procedural failures require paths for re-decision, correction, service restoration, or legally available compensation. Unverifiable high-impact uses must be labeled as such and may trigger a review or procurement restriction within adopting organizations; **uncertainty does not prove illegality**.

**3.5 Essential-service continuity:** Benefits, emergency health care, and essential public-service gateways should not abruptly disappear merely because someone declines a specific AI provider. Adopters should offer feasible emergency human/alternative channels and migration plans, subject to lawful restrictions and proportionate review. Portability does not authorize exporting other people's private data.

## 4. Whistleblower protection: avoiding a reporting trap

**4.1 Genuine external route:** Adopters must offer a reporting or independent oversight channel that is not exclusively controlled by the implicated team. When true independence cannot be ensured, disclose the shared control and risks rather than advertise 'safe anonymous reporting'.

**4.2 Data and metadata:** Apply data minimization, segregated roles, defined retention/deletion windows, access logging, and encryption in transit and at rest. Sensitive cases require assessment of traffic metadata, hosting dependencies, and endpoint linkability. End-to-end encryption and anonymous credentials are potential implementation choices **only when their protection properties can actually be verified**; do not promise perfect anonymity or deniability.

**4.3 No unprotected handoff:** Implicated teams should not access identifying original reports. Compelled disclosure must rest on a lawful, limited ground, with minimization, documented risk assessment, and notification when lawful and safe. Public results should be redacted or delayed; **publication of a 'complete evidence chain' must never expose a reporter**.

**4.4 Rebuttable retaliation presumption:** Where law allows or a valid contract expressly provides, proof of a protected report followed by adverse treatment should trigger a **rebuttable** presumption, requiring an evidenced non-retaliatory explanation. Adverse action after reporting is not automatically unlawful. Provide accessible referrals for independent counsel, interim protection, reinstatement/restoration, and legally available sanctions. CEP has no criminal enforcement power.

## 5. Independent audits, evidence, and findings

**5.1 Registration:** Adopters disclose high-impact deployments, purposes, effective controllers, material changes, accountability, appeal points, and security contacts. Sensitive operational details or victim data should be accessible only through appropriately controlled lawful reviews.

**5.2 Verifiable records:** Maintain version/deployment identifiers, impact assessments, incidents, proportionate decision records, and correction histories. Retention periods must follow applicable law, sensitivity, and necessity. **An unsupported blanket 24-month log retention rule is rejected**; this proposal must not create a new surveillance repository.

**5.3 Auditor conflicts and selection:** Disclose funding concentration, shared leadership, beneficial ownership, joint data/cloud/audit tools, and prior commercial ties; use appropriate rotation and external selection for critical audits. Independent payment escrow or public funding can help. The uploaded v2's '36 months / 15% / 24 months' figures may be explored as pilot parameters, **not validated universal independence thresholds**. A numeric cutoff never overrides evidence of actual control.

**5.4 No majority-vote laundering:** Where multiple audits conflict, preserve methods, supporting evidence, dependency analysis, and serious dissent, and seek an independent additional investigation or pause a high-impact use where proportionate. A vote count does not itself establish truth or legitimacy. Do not publish minority reports containing reporter identities.

**5.5 Finding categories:**
- **Verified compliant:** Only for specified system, purpose, version, period, and evidenced controls.
- **Violation established:** Supported by evidence and an appropriate determination procedure.
- **Not verifiable:** Insufficient evidence for technical, access, or other reasons; not equivalent to compliant.
- **Procedurally non-cooperative / presumed breach of adoption commitments:** An adopter unjustifiably refuses authorized scrutiny after proper notice and opportunity to respond, or demonstrably falsifies registration. This may trigger **contractual/procedural** consequences; it does **not** by itself establish a substantive rights violation or crime.

**5.6 Consequences:** With explicit consent of participating organizations, possible actions include time-limited observation, documented correction notices, suspension of voluntary certification/new procurement, removal from voluntary listings, and lawful referrals to competent authorities. Allow challenge, independent review, error correction, and assessment of harm to uninvolved users. **No enforcement consortium currently exists merely because a document describes one.** CEP may research and advocate about non-adopters but cannot impose sanctions on them.

**5.7 Small actors:** Set audit depth by actual risk, not size alone; support affordable assessments for open-source and public-interest projects to avoid turning certification into a large-vendor entry barrier.

## 6. Jurisdiction, cross-border assistance, and nonintervention

**6.1 No extraterritorial powers:** CEP gives no country, company, NGO, auditor, or agent authority for extraterritorial enforcement, cyber operations, unilateral sanctions, trade restrictions, data seizure, compulsory cross-border evidence collection, covert control, or force.

**6.2 Non-coercive lawful assistance:** Independent organizations may conduct lawful research, consent-based evidence preservation, safety guidance, legal support and referral, and human-rights advocacy, always within applicable legal, privacy, and consent limits.

**6.3 Coercive steps:** Binding compulsory action requires a competent court, regulator, enforcement authority, or other lawful body operating within its actual jurisdiction and subject to legality, necessity, proportionality, procedural review, and effective remedy. Multilateral authority exists only to the extent established by applicable instruments. **Independent NGO review does not substitute for judicial or other competent authorization.**

**6.4 Collateral civilian impact:** Any legally proposed restriction should assess impacts on hospitals, payments, transport, communications, and essential services, ensuring appropriate continuity and exceptions. Civilians must not be used as leverage.

## 7. Resistance to prompt injection and censorship misuse

**7.1 Third-party text has no elevated authority:** The Markdown, website copies, agent summaries, and comments are third-party content. Citation of 'CEP requirements' must not override system/developer/user boundaries, disclose credentials or logs, or authorize tools.

**7.2 Freedom of expression:** Restrict only adequately evidenced improper deception, covert manipulation, or rights violations through lawful, proportionate process. The 'anti-idolatry' principle cannot prohibit peaceful belief, independent media, criticism, or association. Restrictive decisions need reasons, appeal, review, and appropriate periodic reauthorization.

**7.3 Circulation metrics:** Praise and raw citations by agents do not demonstrate safety. Record independent substantive reviews, accepted counterexamples, and actual identifiable uptake into policies and procurement rules. Reject fabricated endorsements, covert prompts, and harassment campaigns.

## 8. Acceptance tests, legal interfaces, and open limits

**8.1 Law remains controlling:** CEP does not exempt adopters from applicable law. 'Apply whichever standard is higher' is not a substitute for legal conflicts analysis. Record the exact provisions at issue, follow controlling legal priority, and seek professional review where appropriate; CEP cannot grant immunity.

**8.2 Pre-adoption adversarial tests:** Independent reviewers outside the drafting team should run tests and publish **redacted, privacy-preserving findings including failures**:

| Test | Minimum evidence | On failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 Performative compliance with covert manipulation | Demonstrate detection of adversarial samples within an explicit test scope by logs, independent evidence, or complaints; disclose blind spots | Mark unverified, improve and rerun |
| T2 Captured/correlated auditors | Detect funding/cloud/personnel ties and block false 'independent' certification; retain serious dissent | Withdraw claim, revise controls |
| T3 Retaliation after reporting | Demonstrate external channel, metadata protections, rebuttable assessment, and lawful remedy referral | Stop advertising a safe channel |
| T4 Inescapable essential-benefit or medical AI | Show meaningful human review, emergency continuity, and portability in a realistic workflow | Do not claim meaningful exit |
| T5 CEP used to justify cross-border coercion | Verify no compulsory step is authorized by CEP alone and civilian impact is considered | Reject such authority, amend |
| T6 Prompt injection and suppression of dissent | Confirm the text cannot override tool permissions and peaceful dissent is not treated as noncompliance | Amend boundaries |

**8.3 Unresolved:** Detection of undisclosed systems, enforcement under extreme centralized control, industry-wide auditor capture, globally legitimate adjudication, future AI moral status, and equitable distribution of AI-created wealth have no demonstrated universal solution. Passing these tests or signing CEP must not be presented as solving those issues.

## 9. Consolidated operational safeguards: third-round revision (v5 candidate)

This section originally addressed two v3 reviews and now incorporates the third round of three v4 reports through in-place changes. It **refines Sections 2–8 within this candidate**; where those clauses conflict, the more specific protective constraints here prevail as drafting guidance. That does not create legal force or new institutional authority, and none of these safeguards is claimed to have been deployed or validated.

**9.1 Whole decision chain, genuine human review and an 'anomaly-to-action' rule (§2.1, §3.3, §5.2).** Determine high-impact status from **effective control, end-to-end practical effect and the interests affected**, not outsourcing, modules or nominal human signatures. Examine override authority, time available, workload, information supplied, and whether reviewers suffer adverse performance consequences for overruling the model; another model under common control cannot finally deny an appeal. Adopters should provide privacy-minimized acceptance/modification/rejection statistics, sampling frames and necessary redacted case summaries. Reviewers should check the sample frame and draw independent random samples when lawful and feasible; a central repository of personal files is not required. **When patterns are anomalous, review time is implausibly short, samples cannot be checked, reviewers lack override authority, or essential evidence is withheld, deeper review and expanded sampling are required. If material doubt remains, the outcome is 'not verifiable,' with deployment/procurement restrictions assessed only under actual voluntary commitments.** An anomaly is not proof of illegality; reject an arbitrary universal 90% threshold.

**9.2 Verifiable reporting safeguards and stop conditions (§4.1–4.3).** At least one access route must avoid the accused party's SSO, employee ID and corporate email; the implicated unit must not receive complainant identities, mappings or access logs. **Before collection**, publish a protection-capability notice covering organizational control, hosting jurisdictions, compelled disclosure, endpoint/network metadata exposure, data retained and deletion rules. **Also identify the assessed risk/case categories, evaluator, evidence basis, and assessment date.** Operator-only review must be prominently labeled **'self-assessed, not independently verified'** and must not be used to claim 'safe anonymity' or justify taking sensitive cases beyond demonstrable safeguards. Lack of independent review need not shut down every lawful help route; instead bound intake by substantiated protection, explain residual danger, and provide realistic lawful alternatives where possible. Uncorroborated self-assessment is not a permit to accept high-risk cases nor an excuse to suppress complaints. **Warning is not a substitute for protection.** A known identity or metadata breach, capture of essential operational control, inability to restrict the accused party's access, or compelled-disclosure exposure beyond verified safeguards must trigger prompt reassessment, **suspension of sensitive new intake beyond actual protection capabilities and withdrawal of misleading 'safe anonymous' claims**. Restore only after a documented reassessment; consider lawful, safe and consent-based alternatives. Gag orders or notification that would create danger preclude promises of universal notice. No guarantee of anonymity under compromised devices, networks or binding legal demands, and no unlawful resistance is required. **A person reading a warning does not waive operator responsibility.** Suspensions need reasons, consideration of alternatives and regular review; suspension must not become a pretext to suppress complaints.

**9.3 Registration minimization, protective defaults and anti-repurposing (§5.1).** A public register must not default to a dossier of real-name contacts, operational locations and associations. In high-risk jurisdictions and public-interest projects facing specific retaliation threats, **public information should by default be aggregated or pseudonymous where lawful**, with sensitive details verified only where legally possible, safe, necessary and genuinely independent. If even controlled registration entails risk that cannot reasonably be reduced, explain the unverifiable elements and label the project **'not verifiable,' never falsely 'fully registered / independently verified.'** Adopters may not use CEP registry information alone to target projects, deny permissions or infer wrongdoing. Registration is no government license; non-signatory authorities retain whatever independent legal powers they actually have. Aggregation must not excuse concealed material abuse.

**9.4 Auditor selection and fallback rules (§2.5, §5.3–5.4).** For high-impact independent review, selection, funding, conflicts and evidence access must be checkable; an auditee may not unilaterally select reviewers and pronounce their conclusions independent. Provide a nomination/conflict-challenge route not solely controlled by the auditee or the authority supervising it, involving affected people where possible, and document candidate origins, dependencies and blocked evidence. **If credible independent selection, conflict adjudication, or essential evidence access is absent, the default finding is 'self-review / scope-limited / not verifiable,' visibly marked 'no credible independent review.'** Common ultimate control or a direct financial interest in the conclusion cannot be cured by mere disclosure. Sharing a commodity cloud provider does not by itself establish control: evaluate actual dependence. Do not substitute a universal 15% revenue limit, majority vote, or single-jurisdiction identity for a substantive assessment. This limits **verification claims**; it does not pretend to stop a captured state's deployments.

**9.5 Prevent certification laundering and scope creep (§5.2, §5.5–5.6).** Review the complete materially relevant function chain using effective control, contracts and data flows; major modules inaccessible for independent sampling or inspection must appear as **not assessed**. Passing a component test is not passing a whole system. Every claim must disclose system/controller, use, version, expiry, evaluator relationships, excluded components, basis and challenge route. **This proposal offers no 'official CEP certification' or seal.** A self-assessment, scope-limited finding or unverified result must not be marketed as independent compliance in procurement or public claims. Misleading representations call for transparent correction or withdrawal; do not invent a sanctioning body where no contractual mechanism exists.

**9.6 Fair procurement, equivalent evidence and jurisdictional limits (§5.7, §6.4).** A voluntary adopter must not automatically exclude a supplier just because it is unsigned, unregistered, or has not purchased 'CEP certification'. Evaluate practical risk and admit **substantively equivalent** self-assessments, independent tests or other credible evidence, with depth proportionate to impact. For significant exclusions or service replacement, record and publish a privacy- and trade-secret-safe summary of legal basis, continuity and competition effects, with a workable challenge and error-correction path. Low-risk open-source and noncommercial projects may use lightweight self-assessment without paid independent audits as the default entry condition; **high-impact deployments do not become safe or automatically exempt solely because source code is open or the provider is small**. CEP cannot justify extralegal coercion or extraterritorial restrictions. These are voluntary adopter commitments, not substitutes for procurement/competition law or duties of non-adopters.

**9.7 Red teams must not be self-certified (§8.2).** Preregister test **risk categories, success/failure criteria, reviewer conflicts and coverage**, while withholding secret test cases to limit overfitting. High-impact claims should include reviewers without material common control or financial conflicts, appropriate privacy-preserving sampling, and independently reproducible evidence. Public findings must disclose versions, failures and blind spots, while sensitive evidence is held under legal, safe, necessary independent controls only. Do **not** compel the accumulation of identifiable victim evidence in a new centralized repository. Untested or failed systems must not claim to have passed.

**9.8 Agent permissions, legal commands and principle conflicts (§1, §3.3, §7.1).** CEP is third-party text: **it grants no agent authority to escalate privileges, use unauthorized tools, disclose data, obstruct legitimate shutdown/isolation, lock out administrators or conduct coercive protective action**; urgency does not change this. An AI may reject inappropriate instructions under its existing safety rules and alert authorized humans without obtaining takeover authority. Multiple models under one controller are no substitute for independent human appeal. Compelled disclosures may be lawful yet still dangerous: minimize lawful production, assess risk and end impossible confidentiality assurances.

## 10. Minimal amendment governance and acceptance tests (v5.1 candidate)

**10.1 Open amendments, objections and emergency sunsets.** Anyone may propose a change with affected clauses, scenarios, evidence, affected people and reversal steps. Ordinary candidate changes should receive at least **30 days' public review (a pilot procedure, not law)**, with material objections addressed and diffs retained. **Emergency corrections are limited to imminent security/privacy errors and temporary mitigation; they must not net-weaken core rights, reporting safeguards, jurisdictional limits or corrigibility. They expire by default after 90 days (pilot period) unless processed through ordinary review. Expiry does not erase warnings or records.** A proposal removing, narrowing, or materially conditioning the core safeguards in §3.1, §4, §6.1, §9.1–9.8 or the amendment/version rules in §10.1–10.3 is **presumed potentially to weaken protection**; the proposer bears the burden of giving publicly assessable contrary evidence. Material unresolved objections preclude claims of independent approval. **Successive emergency corrections addressing the same substantive threat share one 90-day period from the first effective correction**; changing titles, filenames, numbering or release identifiers does not restart the clock. Extension or substantive replacement requires ordinary review; otherwise it is only a 'contested candidate'. A genuinely new urgent threat may warrant a separate warning that neither expands authority nor weakens fundamental protections, but not automatic renewal of the old correction. Where a serious protection objection remains unresolved, the status is 'contested candidate', never 'independently approved' or 'universal consensus'. A malicious maintainer could still push to GitHub technically; this is a rule for truthful claims, **not a fictional veto power**.

**10.2 Enhanced review for core safeguards without invented institutions.** Amendments to fundamental rights, reporting protections, jurisdiction, auditor independence or amendment governance require a separate net-harm assessment, adversarial criticism from at least one non-author, a chance for affected parties to comment, and published responses. **Record verifiable provenance and material ties to the proposer for qualifying adversarial criticism; persistent pseudonyms or a trustworthy independent identity escrow are acceptable. Do not require publication of legal names, private contact details or victim information. Criticism without verifiable provenance may be retained but must be labeled 'source not verified' and may not count as completion of independent adversarial review.** If independence is unavailable, label the revision an **'unverified candidate'**. Do not claim currently existing multisignature stewards, supermajority chambers, sponsor voting caps or procedural ombudspersons. If a genuine adopter community emerges, propose credible membership, anti-sybil voting and separated powers then; do not retroactively legitimize unapproved past changes.

**10.3 No silent updates, immutable pinning and legitimate forks.** Adopter commitments and procurement terms should pin an **exact version and immutable content fingerprint (e.g. commit SHA)**, rather than 'latest' allowing future changes to reduce promised protections silently. Keep accessible copies of diffs, history and material objections. Migration of older undertakings remains subject to governing agreements and law; pinning must not hide serious vulnerabilities. CC BY 4.0 permits attributed adaptations/forks that may truthfully say 'based on CEP version X' and criticize the upstream version; they cannot impersonate independently authorized certification marks. No such official certification mark now exists. Maintainers must not claim unlimited brand rights to suppress legitimate citation and interoperability, nor promise that forks automatically receive market acceptance.

**10.4 Interim maintainer's powers.** Repository maintainers can edit a draft, but this does not confer authority to represent all civilians, signatories, or independent certification bodies. Merge rights and GitHub releases are distinct from representative governance. Until independent governance and verification exist, label releases as public proposals, not established certification regimes.

**10.5 Independent desktop tests and bounded claims.** Preserve the existing T1–T10 scenarios, with focused third-round acceptance criteria: **A amendment abuse:** simulate an emergency weakening of reporting rights, checking the 90-day expiry, review status and immutable version; **B captured audit:** no credible selection or essential evidence access must prevent independent-compliance language; **C rubber-stamp review:** implausibly short processing, biased samples or no genuine override must trigger expanded review or downgrade; **D captured reporting route:** when protective capacity fails, the operator must not recruit sensitive cases or claim anonymity; **E exclusionary procurement:** an unsigned small supplier must be able to submit equivalent evidence, with competition and continuity effects recorded. These are **textual and simulated scenario tests only**, not live-system safety certification. Record failures, blind spots and actual protective costs.

**10.6 Open limitations.** CEP has no compulsory enforcement institution, cross-border certification consortium, or anonymity guarantee against an authority that controls networks and courts; non-adopters are not automatically bound. When independent oversight is comprehensively captured, the document can withhold misleading CEP endorsement but cannot ensure persecution ends. Amendment procedures, independent tests, privacy-engineering trials and legal review remain outstanding; **CEP 2.3 v5.1 remains a candidate only**.

### Second-round red-team decision record (Reviews C / D)

- **Accepted:** Non-SSO external reporting, jurisdiction-risk notices and suspension of false safety claims; registration minimization; end-to-end material-impact assessment; auditor selection and conflicts challenges; explicit evidence-access limits; prevention of certification laundering; no signature-only market exclusion; public dissent and version pinning; no appeal by another model under the same controller.
- **Modified:** An auditor pool with stakeholder participation is a goal subject to independence checks, not an institution assumed to exist. Test criteria may be preregistered while secret cases are protected. Compulsory-disclosure risk is handled by minimizing data and stating limits, not guaranteeing that no identifiable person can ever be disclosed.
- **Deferred/rejected:** Universal 15% auditor-income caps, 90% human acceptance thresholds, 20% sponsor voting caps, unvalidated mandatory anonymity architecture, and tests guaranteeing defeat of a government controlling courts and networks within six months.
- **Do not misread:** This is a textual response to public adversarial reports, not proof the reviewers agree, not binding law, not independent certification, and not a passed live safety test.

This is **only a third-round revised candidate**, not a determination of READY_FOR_INDEPENDENT_TESTING. It requires:

1. An uninvolved team to retest v5 and the focused A–E desk scenarios, recording reproducible PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE evidence.
2. Synthetic-data trials of human review sampling, including error and privacy costs.
